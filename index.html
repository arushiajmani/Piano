Spectrogram

A spectrogram is a picture of the sound over time. Each key plays a WAV sample. After your first note, that audio is routed through a Web Audio AnalyserNode (FFT size 2048). Every animation frame, a new column is drawn on the right and the older picture slides left.

  • Up / down: frequency on a log scale — high pitches at the top (~20 kHz), low at the bottom (~20 Hz).
  • Left / right: time, newest energy on the right edge.
  • Color: how much energy is in that frequency bin. Brighter means louder in that band.

A piano tone is not one line. You should see the fundamental (the named pitch, e.g. A4 = 440 Hz) plus quieter harmonics stacked above it. That is why a real sample looks richer than a sine wave.

The strip below is a Greenwood-style map of the uncoiled basilar membrane: low frequencies peak toward the apex (left), high toward the base (right). Play C4, then E5, and the marker jumps toward the base — place pitch.

This is a software model of the audio already playing in the browser. It is not a room microphone, not an EEG or BCI, and not a clinical hearing test.
